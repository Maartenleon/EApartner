Track 1

Architecture
for Transformation

This is enterprise architecture, done inside your transformation. It bridges direction and execution — and stays close to delivery so the bridge doesn't drift. Strategy, capabilities, data, applications, and technology — held coherent enough that decisions compound, modular enough that change doesn't ripple, while the organisation moves. The output isn't a model. It's the decisions your transformation needs next.

Most transformations don't fail for lack of effort. They fail because reasonable decisions, made in different layers and different domains, stop adding up. These are the moments enterprise architecture is for.

Sound familiar?

How do I add new systems without creating an integration spaghetti down the line?

Without architecture guardrails, every new system becomes a new integration point. I define integration principles, a canonical data model, and a target architecture that new systems slot into — before they're built, not after the damage is done.

How do I decide which tech initiatives to prioritise when everything feels urgent?

Most organisations have more initiatives than focus. I map your capability gaps to strategic goals and use that as the basis for sequencing — so every initiative you fund has a clear architectural reason for coming before the others.

Which of our processes are actually suitable for AI, and which ones aren't ready yet?

AI readiness is architectural: it depends on data quality, capability ownership, and integration maturity. I assess your landscape layer by layer and give you a clear picture of where agents can deliver value today — and where the foundational work comes first.

Every programme creates its own architecture — how do we get coherence without a central bottleneck?

When architecture decisions are made programme by programme, coherence only comes from luck or conflict. I establish shared principles and a reference architecture that programmes inherit — so alignment happens by design, not constant negotiation.

The model

Enterprise architecture,
in one picture

Coherence has two axes. Vertically, every layer constrains and enables the one above and below — strategy that doesn't reach technology is wishful thinking, technology disconnected from strategy is debt waiting to happen. Horizontally, every business domain has to fit the same model, or integration becomes the work instead of the outcome. AI and agents cut across all of it.

But coherence alone makes an organisation rigid. Modularity is the second discipline — capabilities, data domains, applications, and platforms designed as parts that can be replaced, repriced, or reorganised without rebuilding the whole. Architecture's job is to hold both: structure tight enough that decisions compound, loose enough that change in one place doesn't cascade everywhere.

Six layers

Strategy
to AI

Each layer carries a decision the organisation can't afford to leave implicit. The artefacts below are what carry those decisions — small, sharp, and built to be useful at the moment someone has to act on them.

01

Strategy

The decision: what this organisation will and won't do, structurally, over the horizon that matters — including the principles that govern security, risk, and compliance before they become someone else's emergency.
The artefact: a small set of architectural principles with real teeth, constraints individual projects can't quietly negotiate away, and a North Star that survives reorganisation.

02

Capabilities

The decision: where to invest, where to consolidate, where to leave alone — and which value streams each capability has to serve.
The artefact: a capability map with domain ownership, maturity scored honestly, and change prioritised against capability gaps rather than initiative momentum.

03

Data

The decision: who owns what, which system is the source of truth, and what "the customer" actually means across the organisation.
The artefact: a conceptual data model with quality definitions, ownership, and the acceptance criteria AI needs to be reliable rather than confident-sounding.

04

Applications

The decision: which systems stay, which go, which get rebuilt, and in what order — before the wrong sequence gets funded.
The artefact: an application landscape that surfaces redundancy, integration complexity, and the right modernisation sequence anchored to capability priorities.

05

Platforms & Infrastructure

The decision: the foundational bets — cloud, data platform, integration layer — that constrain everything above for years.
The artefact: a technology radar, build-vs-buy principles, and debt mapping that makes the right bets visible before they become irreversible.

06

AI / Agents

The decision: where autonomous action is sanctioned, what agents can access, trigger, and decide, and where human oversight is required.
The artefact: an agentic operating model that runs across all five layers as a cross-cutting concern — making clear where AI compounds value and where it compounds risk.

What you walk away with

A working spine,
not a binder

A target architecture that ties strategy to technology through capabilities, data, and applications — coherent enough that decisions compound, modular enough that the next change doesn't require a rebuild. A sequenced roadmap where each step traces to a decision. A small set of principles and guardrails that hold when no one is watching.

And what stays. Architecture that sets direction and walks away is just expensive advice. The work continues close to delivery — catching drift before it becomes debt, adjusting the target when the landscape genuinely shifts (and refusing to when it hasn't), and staying in the room until the path forward is obvious without me. Direction without presence is how transformations quietly lose their spine.
