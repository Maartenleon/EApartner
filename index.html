Why enterprise architecture

Every satisfying organisational experience (a product that works, a platform that scales, a decision that sticks) is held together by architecture most people never see.

The pattern is familiar: too many initiatives, too little focus. An IT landscape that tangles as the organisation grows. Strategic direction that doesn't reach delivery. Delivery that drifts without strategic direction. The need isn't more technology. It's coherence across the whole.

Architecture earns its place close enough to strategy to shape decisions, and close enough to delivery to govern what actually gets built. That means two things need to work:
transformations need solid architecture to steer them, and the architecture function itself needs to deliver. How that looks depends on where you are.

How I work

Approach

Every engagement follows the same rhythm: a sharp diagnostic, a clear architecture, then close involvement during delivery. The content differs by track. The discipline doesn't.

01

Diagnose, 4 weeks

QuickScan · Maturity Audit

No assumptions, no predefined answers. For transformation programmes: where does complexity sit, what is blocking progress, which architectural decisions are urgent? For EA functions: where are the six capability gaps? The output is a prioritised map of what needs to happen, not a thick report.

02

Architect, 4 weeks

Target State · Roadmap · Decisions

Translate the diagnosis into a clear architecture. Trade-offs get made, options get documented, a roadmap gets set, with guardrails not just milestones. Every artefact earns its place because it changes what someone does next. Decisions over documents.

03

Steer, ongoing

Embedded Partner · Delivery Support

Architecture doesn't survive first contact with delivery unchanged. Steer is the embedded partner phase: guardrails, course corrections when the landscape shifts, and a thinking partner close enough to the work to catch drift before it becomes debt. Until the path forward stays obvious without me in the room.
