Why enterprise architecture

Enterprise architecture creates the structure that lets organisations grow, transform, and automate — without losing coherence. It works on two axes at once.

Every strategic choice should trace a clear line down through capabilities, data, applications, and technology. When these layers are connected, decisions at the top become executable at the bottom — and constraints at the bottom feed back up before they become surprises.

Most complexity isn't inside a domain — it lives in the seams between them. Shared capabilities, data ownership, integration patterns, reusable platforms. Architecture creates the coherence across domains that prevents every team from solving the same problem differently.

The result: an organisation that controls complexity instead of being controlled by it — while staying ready for growth, transformation, and an increasingly automated future.

How I work

Approach

Every engagement starts with a sharp diagnostic — no assumptions, no predefined answers. From there: a clear architecture. Then close involvement during delivery, until the path forward stays obvious without me in the room.

01

Diagnose — 1 month

QuickScan · Maturity Audit

A 4 week diagnostic. For transformation programmes: where does complexity sit, what is blocking progress, which architectural decisions are urgent? For EA functions: where are the six capability gaps? Output is a prioritised map of what needs to happen next — not a thick report.

02

Architect — 1 month

Target State · Decisions · Roadmap

Translate the diagnosis into a clear architecture. Trade-offs get made, options get documented, a roadmap gets set — with guardrails, not just milestones. Decisions over documents: every artefact earns its place because it changes what someone does next.

03

Steer — 1 month

Embedded Partner · Delivery Support

Architecture does not survive first contact with delivery unchanged. Steer is the embedded partner phase: guardrails, course corrections when the landscape shifts, and a thinking partner close enough to the work to catch drift before it becomes debt.
