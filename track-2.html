Track 2

Architecture
as a Function

The EA function is not a role — it is a set of capabilities. Each can be assessed, prioritised, and deliberately improved. This is how you build an architecture function that compounds in value over time.

The model

Six capabilities,
one coherent function

An architecture function delivers value through six distinct capabilities. Each can be independently assessed on a five-level maturity scale — and independently improved. The bar shows a typical starting profile. The target boundary shows where each capability needs to reach.

Current state
Target state

01

Capability 1

Direction-
setting

The mandate, vision, and principles that give the EA function its own north star.

The capability

The architecture function needs its own direction before it can give direction to others. This means defining the mandate — what EA is responsible for and what it is not — and expressing a long-term architecture vision that orients every decision made in its name.

Without this clarity, EA becomes reactive: responding to project requests, firefighting integration problems, and producing artefacts no one uses. With it, the function has a purpose it can be held accountable to.

  • Defined EA mandate, scope, and explicit non-goals
  • Architecture vision aligned to business strategy
  • Principles that set real boundaries on design decisions
  • Annual review cycle anchored to strategic planning

Maturity indicators

L1 — Ad hoc: Principles exist in a document no one reads. The EA mandate is unclear, or varies depending on who you ask.

L3 — Managed: A documented vision is actively referenced in architecture reviews. Principles have measurable criteria.

L4 — Integrated: The architecture vision shapes every major investment decision. Direction is co-owned with leadership and refreshed annually.

02

Capability 2

Governance

Decision rights, review processes, and the accountability that keeps architecture authoritative.

The capability

Governance is how architecture authority is structured and exercised. Without it, architectural decisions happen by default — whoever builds first sets the standard. Good governance is not about adding process; it is about making the right decisions at the right level with the right accountability.

The absence of governance does not mean freedom. It means architecture accumulates by accident, and the cost of the resulting inconsistency falls on the teams that come after.

  • Clear decision rights per architectural domain
  • Defined review triggers — what requires architecture sign-off
  • Architecture Decision Records with rationale and alternatives
  • Escalation path for conflicting architectural positions

Maturity indicators

L1 — Ad hoc: Reviews happen ad hoc and slow things down. Nobody knows what requires review or who decides.

L2 — Defined: A review process exists and is documented. Criteria for what triggers review are clear, though inconsistently applied.

L3 — Managed: Governance adds clarity rather than friction. Teams know what needs review, decisions are documented, and the process is widely seen as helpful.

03

Capability 3

Landscape
Intelligence

Visibility of the current and target state across all architecture layers — maintained, not just documented.

The capability

You can only govern what you can see. The architecture landscape gives the EA function — and the organisation — a shared map: not just of systems, but of capabilities, data flows, integration dependencies, and target states.

Without it, every project starts from scratch, duplication stays invisible, and the cumulative effect of local decisions only becomes apparent when it is already expensive to undo.

  • Current state view across capabilities, applications, data, and technology
  • Target architecture per domain with versioning and ownership
  • Dependency map that sequences and de-risks change
  • Regular refresh cycle with domain owner accountability

Maturity indicators

L1 — Ad hoc: No documented landscape exists. Knowledge is tribal and lives with individuals.

L2 — Defined: A landscape exists but is treated as a project deliverable — created once and not maintained.

L3 — Managed: The landscape is a living asset, referenced in planning sessions, kept current by domain owners, and used to sequence and prioritise change.

04

Capability 4

Standards
& Patterns

Reusable decisions that prevent reinvention and make architectural coherence the path of least resistance.

The capability

Every time a team makes an architectural decision without a standard to refer to, they either reinvent the wheel or contradict what another team just decided. Standards and patterns are how the EA function makes good decisions repeatable — and bad ones harder.

They are not a bureaucratic constraint. They are encoded knowledge: the distillation of past decisions, past failures, and accumulated experience — made available at the moment someone needs it.

  • Technology standards per domain — vetted, versioned, rationale included
  • Reusable patterns for common architectural problems
  • Exception process that feeds back into the standards
  • Adoption tracking to distinguish standards that stick from those that don't

Maturity indicators

L1 — Ad hoc: Conventions exist but are unwritten. Standards are transmitted verbally and inconsistently.

L2 — Defined: Standards are documented and accessible. But adoption is not tracked and exceptions are unmanaged.

L4 — Integrated: Teams pull from a living pattern library before starting design. Exceptions are tracked and feed back into the standards. Non-standard decisions are conscious and documented.

05

Capability 5

Engagement
& Advisory

How the architecture function stays connected to the work — present, useful, and never just a checkpoint.

The capability

Architecture adds value when it is present, not just when it reviews. Engagement is how the EA function stays connected to the work: embedded in projects early, visible in planning, and genuinely useful in real time — not waiting to be asked.

A function that only shows up to approve or reject builds a reputation for slowing things down. One that is embedded from the start builds a reputation for making things better.

  • Clear support model — who gets architect involvement, and when
  • Early engagement criteria for projects and programmes
  • Architecture community of practice for cross-functional alignment
  • Regular communication of architecture intent to stakeholders

Maturity indicators

L1 — Ad hoc: EA is called in when something has already gone wrong. Involvement is reactive and often unwelcome.

L3 — Managed: Engagement criteria are defined. Architecture is brought into projects at the right stage, consistently.

L4 — Integrated: Architects are embedded in strategic initiatives from the start. Teams request architecture support rather than being mandated to accept it.

06

Capability 6

Portfolio
Intelligence

Architecture connected to investment decisions — where the function becomes accountable for the return on technology.

The capability

Architecture is only as powerful as its connection to where money flows. Portfolio intelligence is how the EA function makes the landscape visible to investment decisions — showing where duplication creates cost, where gaps block strategic value, and where the sequencing of investments creates or destroys future options.

Without this connection, architecture produces insight that does not change anything. With it, architectural quality becomes a measurable business outcome.

  • Architecture view as standard input to investment decisions
  • Technical debt quantified, prioritised, and tracked over time
  • Investment sequencing based on architecture dependencies
  • Benefits tracking connected to architecture intent

Maturity indicators

L1 — Ad hoc: Architecture and portfolio management operate in parallel without connecting. Investment decisions are made without an architecture perspective.

L2 — Defined: Architecture input is available to portfolio management but not systematically used. The connection depends on individual relationships.

L3 — Managed: No major investment decision is made without an architecture perspective. The EA function is accountable for the architectural coherence of the portfolio.

The cycle

Maturity is a
direction, not a
destination

Each capability can be independently assessed, prioritised, and improved. Together, they define how the EA function matures over time.

01

Audit

Structured assessment of current maturity per capability. Uses defined indicators and concrete evidence — not self-assessment. Identifies strengths and gaps without judgment.

02

Prioritise

Not every capability needs improvement at the same time. Prioritise based on strategic urgency, current pain, and what improvement in one area unlocks in others.

03

Plan

A targeted improvement roadmap per capability: specific interventions, clear ownership, defined milestones, and measurable success criteria.

04

Improve & Reassess

Deliver the plan. Reassess at a defined interval. Adjust based on what has changed in the business and what the next constraint has become.
